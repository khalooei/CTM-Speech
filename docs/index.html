Not all token flips matter

Measuring functional fragility of neural speech tokenizers: small perturbations flip most discrete tokens, but downstream speech recognition breaks because of a small, structured subset of them.

TL;DR

Instability is not fragility.

Token change rate counts how many discrete tokens a perturbation flips. We ask a different question: which flipped tokens actually carry the downstream damage? On DAC, Mimi and SpeechTokenizer, the answer is a small, structured minority.

Method

Functional critical token mass (F-CTM)

Restore a subset of the changed cells to their clean values, decode, and ask how much of the downstream loss gap comes back. F-CTMα is the smallest prefix of a ranking of the changed set that recovers a share α.

Rec(S) = 𝓛corr − 𝓛(g(Z′(S)))𝓛corr − 𝓛clean,   Rec(∅) = 0,   Rec(𝒟) = 1
01Keep 𝒟 whole

Every ranking is a permutation of all changed cells, so full restoration returns the clean codes and Rec(𝒟) = 1 by construction.

02Frames, then cells

Influence is measured per changed frame (≤ T decodes), then per cell inside the 16 most influential frames (≤ 256 decodes).

03Rank ≠ score

Cells are ranked by one recognizer and curves are scored by two held-out ones, removing the winner's-curse inflation of in-sample attribution.

Findings

What the protocol reveals

01 · INSTABILITY ≠ FRAGILITY

Counting flips does not track harm.

Every corruption flips most cells, yet the damage rate ranges from 6% (brown noise) to 66% (CTC-PGD). Once corruption family and codec are fixed, neither TCR (p = 0.88) nor ΔWER (p = 0.72) predicts how concentrated the damage is.

02 · CONCENTRATION

A few cells carry the failure.

The oracle needs a median 9–10% of the changed set to recover 90% of the gap; random restoration needs 30% (DAC) to 70% (Mimi). DAC and SpeechTokenizer are strongly redundant (any random half recovers ~70%); Mimi's 12.5 Hz cells are close to additive.

03 · STRUCTURE

The RVQ hierarchy and the frame.

Coarse stages carry the function: stage 0 alone recovers 0.52 / 0.54 / 0.97 of the gap (DAC / Mimi / SpeechTokenizer). Later stages restored alone are often harmful. Cell-level refinement adds only +0.004 AURC over frame-level restoration: the frame is the natural unit.

04 · ORACLE-FREE PROXIES

Stage order and displacement come close.

RVQ-early ordering is within 0.03 AURC of the oracle on DAC and SpeechTokenizer; projected codeword displacement is the best proxy on Mimi. VQ margin is at or below random: the cells that flip are not the cells that matter.

05 · ADVERSARIAL VS NATURAL

PGD is only modestly more concentrated.

After same-utterance severity matching, CTC-PGD damage has higher AURC than Gaussian noise on DAC (+0.17) and Mimi (+0.08) but not on SpeechTokenizer, and F-CTM90 does not differ: a shift of the whole curve, not a smaller critical set.

06 · SELECTION BIAS, MEASURED

Why held-out scoring matters.

Scoring with the ranker itself inflates recovery at 5% from 0.64–0.70 to 0.77–0.81, and the inflation grows with the predicted noise-to-gap term σ√(2 ln|F|)/gap. Pure noise can look like a 1-cell critical set in-sample.

Scope. The downstream function is CTC ASR on reconstructed audio; the oracle uses clean tokens and is a measurement, not a defense; F-CTM is a prefix estimate (an upper bound on the minimal critical set), not a combinatorial minimum. Generalization checks cover LibriSpeech test-other, FLEURS, MUSAN/DEMAND noise, EnCodec, the Qwen3-TTS tokenizer, WavTokenizer and StableToken; their per-utterance results are released too.
Reproduce

From released data to every number, in minutes.

The per-utterance results of all runs are in the repository. All reported tables, figures and theory checks regenerate from them on a CPU in minutes; the full grid re-runs on one GPU in about nine hours.

# environment (uv or pip; pick the CUDA wheel index for your driver) git clone https://github.com/khalooei/CTM-Speech && cd CTM-Speech bash scripts/setup_env.sh && source .venv/bin/activate # 1) all reported tables and figures from the released results (CPU) -> outputs/ bash scripts/reproduce_results.sh # 2) re-run the grid on a GPU and compare against the released rows bash scripts/download_data.sh minimal && bash scripts/download_models.sh bash scripts/run_grid.sh configs/main_hier.yaml python scripts/compare_runs.py runs/main_hier/utterance_results.jsonl results/main_hier.jsonl.gz
